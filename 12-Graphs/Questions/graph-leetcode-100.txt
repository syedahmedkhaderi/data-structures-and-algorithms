ADVICE BASED ON YOUR CURRENT CODE

1. Fix the topological sort bug before Day 21.
   In 03-topological-sort.ipynb, topological_sort_util does:
       for i in self.graph:
   That iterates over every key in the whole graph, not the neighbours of
   the current node. It should be:
       for i in self.graph[val]:
   Your sample output looked plausible by coincidence. Re-run it after the
   fix and confirm the order changes. Also note that with defaultdict,
   self.graph[val] on a sink node silently creates an empty entry while you
   are iterating, which can raise a RuntimeError on bigger inputs. Guard it
   with self.graph.get(val, []).

2. Your BFS in 01-bfs-SSSP.ipynb has no visited set.
   Enqueuing full path lists with no visited check is exponential on graphs
   with cycles and will TLE on LC 127 and 815. Two fixes worth learning:
   store only (node, distance) plus a parent dict and reconstruct the path
   at the end, and mark a node visited at enqueue time, not dequeue time.

3. Add path compression to DisjointSet.
   Your find() recurses without compressing. One line changes the
   complexity class:
       self.parent[val] = self.find(self.parent[val])
       return self.parent[val]
   Without it, LC 839 and 952-style problems will time out. Union by rank
   alone gives O(log n); with compression you get near O(1) amortised.

4. Stop using a sentinel like INF = 9999 in Floyd-Warshall.
   In 01-Floyd-Warshall.ipynb, distance[i][k] + distance[k][j] can add two
   sentinels together and produce a number that looks like a real path.
   Use float('inf') and skip the relaxation when either side is infinite.
   Also, your input matrix has G[3][3] = 1, so the diagonal is wrong. The
   diagonal must be 0 or you are claiming a node is 1 away from itself.

5. Your Floyd-Warshall mutates the caller's matrix.
   distance = G is an alias, not a copy. Use copy.deepcopy(G). You will hit
   this exact class of bug on LC 1462 where the grader reuses inputs.

6. checkRoute in RouteBetweenNodes.py has a broken break.
   The break exits the inner for loop only, so the while loop keeps running
   after you found the path. It happens to return the right answer, but on
   LC 1971 with 200k edges it wastes the whole traversal. Return directly.

7. DFS with an explicit stack gives a different order than recursive DFS.
   In 02-bfs-and-dfs.ipynb your dfs marks visited at push time, which is
   correct for correctness but produces reverse-neighbour ordering. Be aware
   of this when a problem asks for lexicographically smallest output
   (LC 332 will punish you for it).

HOW TO ACTUALLY RUN THE 50 DAYS

  Per question, cap yourself at 25 minutes of thinking before reading a
  hint, and 40 minutes total before reading an editorial. Two questions is
  roughly one hour a day. If you go past that consistently you are grinding,
  not learning.

  Write the solution in your own template, not LeetCode's. Keep one file
  called graph_templates.py with your five canonical routines: BFS on grid,
  BFS on adjacency list, DFS recursive, Kahn's topological sort, Dijkstra
  with heapq, DSU class. Every question you solve should be a small edit to
  one of those six, not a fresh start. By Day 20 you should be able to type
  all six from memory in under ten minutes.

  Redo, don't just move on. On Days 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50, replace one of
  the two new questions with a re-solve of a question from two phases back,
  from a blank file. If you cannot re-solve LC 210 on Day 30 without help,
  you did not learn it on Day 21.

  Keep a one-line log per question: the trap you fell into. Not the
  solution, the trap. "Forgot to mark visited at enqueue." "Used sum instead
  of max in the relaxation." After 100 entries that log is more valuable
  than the 100 solutions.

  Interview signal: the 20 questions that come up most often out of this
  list are 200, 133, 207, 210, 547, 684, 721, 994, 542, 1091, 127, 743,
  787, 399, 1584, 269, 329, 417, 130, 332. If you are ever short on time,
  those are the ones to keep warm.
